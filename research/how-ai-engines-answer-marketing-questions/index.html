    How AI engines answer marketing questions: a citation study

    We asked a live AI engine eight marketing questions and checked every source it named. For six of the eight it never searched, and 21 of the 52 sources it listed do not exist.

    Brave New teamSept 2026 · 5 min read

    • Search
    • AI
    • Measurement
    • Advertising
    • Brand
    CIT

    Method: eight questions covering marketing principles, the history of advertising, neuromarketing, AI search, films and songs about advertising, marketing reference sites and the term retrievable brand, put to Gemini with Google Search grounding on 14 September 2026, one answer each. Every URL the engine named was requested and its status recorded.

    Finding one: live retrieval fired only for the two questions about AI search and marketing reference sites. The evergreen questions were answered from training data, so the sources listed were the model’s memory of the web rather than anything it had just read. Twenty-one of fifty-two returned not found. That was the number that surprised us.

    Finding two: on the questions where the engine did search, it cited platform vendors, trade publications and several small, recently published explainer posts side by side. A new page can enter those answers within an update cycle. A new page on an evergreen topic cannot, whatever its quality.

    What it means: publish the pages on contested, fresh topics first. Treat evergreen reference pages as a search engine play. Verify every citation an engine gives you before repeating it. Limits: one engine, one sample per question. We will repeat the study on other engines and publish the differences.
