    Principles

    The old stores, still worth opening.

    The handful of ideas most marketing still rests on, each with the person and the year it came from.

    All Principles
    4P
    8 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    The 4 Ps of marketing

    Product, price, place, promotion. E. Jerome McCarthy set the frame out in Basic Marketing in 1960, and it is still the first thing most marketers learn.

    Source: McCarthy, Basic Marketing: A Managerial Approach, 1960
    POS
    5 Sep 2026/Brave New team/4 min

    Positioning in marketing

    Al Ries and Jack Trout argued in 1972 that a brand is a position in the prospect’s mind, not a list of features. The book followed in 1981.

    Source: Ries and Trout, Advertising Age, 1972; Positioning, 1981
    SEG
    1 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    Market segmentation

    Wendell Smith’s 1956 paper in the Journal of Marketing separated product differentiation from market segmentation. Marketing has been splitting audiences ever since.

    Source: Smith, Journal of Marketing, 1956
    02

    History of advertising

    How the store was filled, year by year.

    A timeline of the moments that changed how advertising and marketing reach people, from the first agency to the first AI answer. Who invented advertising, when the first commercials ran, and what each change did.

    All History
    1841
    7 Sep 2026/Brave New team/2 min

    1841. Who invented the advertising agency

    Volney B. Palmer opened what is generally regarded as the first advertising agency in the United States, in Philadelphia, selling newspaper space on commission.

    Source: Palmer agency records, Philadelphia, 1841
    1922
    3 Sep 2026/Brave New team/2 min

    1922. The first paid radio commercial

    On 28 August 1922, WEAF in New York aired a ten-minute talk paid for by the Queensboro Corporation to sell apartments in Jackson Heights.

    Source: WEAF broadcast log, 28 August 1922
    1941
    29 Aug 2026/Brave New team/2 min

    1941. The first television commercial

    On 1 July 1941, a Bulova watch spot ran on WNBT New York before a Dodgers and Phillies game. It showed a clock face and a short line of copy.

    Source: WNBT New York, 1 July 1941
    03

    Branding

    The chemistry under the taste.

    How brands are built and how the brain receives them: brand strategy, brand equity, and the neuromarketing studies that are documented.

    All Branding
    fMRI
    6 Sep 2026/Brave New team/4 min

    The Coke and Pepsi brain study (the Pepsi paradox)

    McClure and colleagues, Neuron, 2004. Told which cola they were drinking, people’s stated preference and their brain activity both changed. The brand did the work the taste did not.

    Source: McClure et al., Neuron, 2004
    S1/S2
    2 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    System 1 and System 2 thinking in marketing

    Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow, 2011. Most decisions, including most purchases, run on the fast, automatic system, and the slow one mostly rationalises afterwards.

    Source: Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow, 2011
    SMH
    26 Aug 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    The somatic marker hypothesis

    Antonio Damasio, Descartes’ Error, 1994. Emotion is part of the machinery of choosing, not noise laid over reason. Patients who lost it could not decide.

    Source: Damasio, Descartes’ Error, 1994
    04

    AI marketing

    The field has changed. The work has not.

    What AI marketing, AI SEO, generative engine optimization and answer engine optimization mean, and what changes for a brand when the buyer asks a model instead of a search engine.

    All AI marketing
    AIM
    14 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    What is AI marketing?

    AI marketing is the use of AI systems in marketing work: to research buyers, generate and test creative, target and bid, and to earn a place in the answers AI assistants give when buyers ask for a recommendation.

    Source: Brave New, 2026
    GEO
    13 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

    Generative engine optimization, or GEO, is the practice of writing and structuring content so that generative engines cite it in the answers they produce. The term comes from a 2023 research paper by Pranjal Aggarwal and colleagues.

    Source: Aggarwal et al., arXiv, November 2023
    AEO
    11 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    What is answer engine optimization (AEO)?

    Answer engine optimization, or AEO, is the practice of preparing content so that it is used in a direct answer rather than a list of links. In practice it means answering one question per page, plainly and early.

    Source: Brave New, 2026
    05

    Reputation

    What the store is worth when nobody is looking.

    What a reputation is, how it differs from a brand, how trust is measured, and what the documented cases teach about keeping it.

    All Reputation
    B/R
    14 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    Brand versus reputation: what is the difference?

    A brand is what a company says and shows about itself. A reputation is what others conclude from what it does. One is authored, the other is earned, and in a buying meeting the second one decides.

    Source: Brave New, 2026
    ETB
    12 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    The Edelman Trust Barometer

    An annual survey of trust in business, government, media and NGOs, published by Edelman every year since 2001. It is the most cited public measure of institutional trust.

    Source: Edelman Trust Barometer, 2001 to 2026
    1982
    10 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    1982. The Tylenol case

    After seven people in the Chicago area died from cyanide-laced Tylenol capsules, Johnson & Johnson recalled the product nationwide and introduced tamper-evident packaging. It remains the reference case in crisis communications.

    Source: Johnson & Johnson, 1982
    06

    Readings

    What we bring back from the field.

    Check our readings. The books we like, what each one argues, and one line worth keeping from each.

    All Readings
    HBG
    13 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    How Brands Grow. Byron Sharp (2010)

    The evidence-based case that brands grow by reaching more buyers, not by deepening loyalty. Built on decades of purchase data from the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute.

    Growth comes from being easy to think of and easy to buy, not from being loved by a few.

    Source: Sharp, Oxford University Press, 2010
    OGL
    11 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    Ogilvy on Advertising. David Ogilvy (1983)

    The working manual of the man who built Ogilvy & Mather, on headlines, layouts, research and the difference between advertising that wins awards and advertising that sells.

    The consumer is not a moron. Write as if she were reading over your shoulder.

    Source: Ogilvy, 1983
    POS
    9 Sep 2026/Brave New team/3 min

    Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind. Al Ries and Jack Trout (1981)

    The book that moved the battleground from the product to the prospect’s mind. Own a word, or a place relative to the leader, before someone else does.

    You do not position a product. You position it in a mind that is already full.

    Source: Ries and Trout, 1981

    Thank You for Smoking (2005)

    Jason Reitman’s satire follows a tobacco lobbyist who can argue any side. A film about spin, and about the difference between persuasion and truth.

    Ray Kroc turns a single hamburger stand into McDonald's. The product was the brothers'. The brand, the system and the name became his.

    Source: Directed by Doug Pray, 2009
