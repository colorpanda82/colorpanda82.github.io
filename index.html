    Principles

    The old stores, still worth opening.

    The handful of ideas most marketing still rests on, each with the person and the year it came from.

    All Principles
    8 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/10 min

    The 4 Ps of marketing

    The marketing mix says a marketer's job is to set four controllable variables: product, price, place and promotion. It is a classification, not a theory, and has never been tested as one.

    Source: McCarthy, Basic Marketing: A Managerial Approach, 1960
    5 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/10 min

    Positioning in marketing

    Positioning, as Ries and Trout set it out in 1972, claims that a brand wins by occupying one clear slot in the prospect's memory. The memory half holds up.

    Source: Ries and Trout, Advertising Age, 1972; Positioning, 1981
    1 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/9 min

    Market segmentation

    Market segmentation claims a market is not one demand curve but several, and that a firm does better serving some of them well than all of them adequately. The evidence supports that claim for categories: buyers differ in what they want and how much they use.

    Source: Smith, Journal of Marketing, 1956

    Branding

    The chemistry under the taste.

    How brands are built and how the brain receives them: brand strategy, brand equity, and the neuromarketing studies that are documented.

    All Branding
    fMRI
    6 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/4 min

    The Coke and Pepsi brain study (the Pepsi paradox)

    McClure and colleagues, Neuron, 2004. Told which cola they were drinking, people’s stated preference and their brain activity both changed. The brand did the work the taste did not.

    Source: McClure et al., Neuron, 2004
    S1/S2
    2 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    System 1 and System 2 thinking in marketing

    Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow, 2011. Most decisions, including most purchases, run on the fast, automatic system, and the slow one mostly rationalises afterwards.

    Source: Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow, 2011
    SMH
    26 Aug 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    The somatic marker hypothesis

    Antonio Damasio, Descartes’ Error, 1994. Emotion is part of the machinery of choosing, not noise laid over reason. Patients who lost it could not decide.

    Source: Damasio, Descartes’ Error, 1994
    04

    AI marketing

    The field has changed. The work has not.

    What AI marketing, AI SEO, generative engine optimization and answer engine optimization mean, and what changes for a brand when the buyer asks a model instead of a search engine.

    All AI marketing
    14 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/4 min

    What is AI marketing?

    AI marketing is the use of artificial intelligence systems inside marketing work: to research buyers, generate and test creative, target and bid on media, and to earn a place in the answers AI assistants give when somebody asks for a recommendation. It is a set of tools and a new distribution channel at the same time, and most teams are only treating it as the first.

    Source: Brave New, 2026
    13 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/4 min

    What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

    Generative engine optimization, or GEO, is the practice of writing and structuring content so that generative engines cite it in the answers they produce. The term comes from a 2023 research paper by Pranjal Aggarwal and colleagues at Princeton, the Georgia Institute of Technology, the Allen Institute for AI and IIT Delhi.

    Source: Aggarwal et al., arXiv, November 2023
    11 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/4 min

    What is answer engine optimization (AEO)?

    Answer engine optimization, or AEO, is the practice of preparing content so that it is used in a direct answer rather than in a list of links. In practice it means answering one question per page, plainly and early, in a block that can be lifted out whole and still make sense.

    Source: Brave New, 2026

    Reputation

    What the store is worth when nobody is looking.

    What a reputation is, how it differs from a brand, how trust is measured, and what the documented cases teach about keeping it.

    All Reputation
    14 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/4 min

    Brand versus reputation: what is the difference?

    A brand is what a company says and shows about itself. A reputation is what other people conclude from what it does. One is authored, the other is earned, and in a buying meeting the second one decides.

    Source: Brave New, 2026
    12 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/4 min

    The Edelman Trust Barometer

    An annual survey of trust in four institutions, business, government, media and non-governmental organisations, published by the communications firm Edelman every year since 2001. It is the most widely cited public measure of institutional trust, and it is worth understanding both what it shows and what it is.

    Source: Edelman Trust Barometer, 2001 to 2026
    10 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/5 min

    1982. The Tylenol case

    In the autumn of 1982, seven people in the Chicago area died after taking Extra-Strength Tylenol capsules that had been laced with potassium cyanide. Johnson & Johnson pulled the product nationally, warned the public not to take it, and reintroduced it in tamper-evident packaging. It remains the reference case in crisis communications, and it is routinely misread.

    Source: Johnson & Johnson, 1982
    06

    Readings

    What we bring back from the field.

    Check our readings. The books we like, what each one argues, and one line worth keeping from each.

    All Readings
    13 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/4 min

    How Brands Grow. Byron Sharp (2010)

    The evidence-based case that brands grow by reaching more buyers rather than by deepening loyalty among the ones they have. Built on decades of purchase panel data from the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, and the most disruptive marketing book of the last twenty years.

    Growth comes from being easy to think of and easy to buy, not from being loved by a few.

    Source: Sharp, Oxford University Press, 2010
    11 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    Ogilvy on Advertising. David Ogilvy (1983)

    The working manual of the man who built Ogilvy & Mather, on headlines, layouts, research, and the difference between advertising that wins awards and advertising that sells. It is a book of craft instructions, not a theory, and it should be read as one.

    The consumer is not a moron. Write as if she were reading over your shoulder.

    Source: Ogilvy, 1983
    9 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind. Al Ries and Jack Trout (1981)

    The book that moved the battleground from the product to the prospect's mind. Own a word, or own a place relative to the leader, before somebody else does. It began as a series of articles in Advertising Age in 1972 and has shaped how marketers talk ever since.

    You do not position a product. You position it in a mind that is already full.

    Source: Ries and Trout, 1981

    Research

    What we went out and measured ourselves.

    Original studies from the Hive: how AI engines answer marketing questions, what people type into a search box, and what that means for a brand. Every number comes from a named tool on a stated date.

    All Research
    14 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/4 min

    How the AI search vocabulary moved in a year

    Three terms compete to name the same worry: that buyers are asking an AI assistant instead of a search engine. We tracked all three in US search volume across a year. AI SEO is the largest, generative engine optimization peaked early and fell back, and answer engine optimization climbed from almost nothing to a real number. Our own data, fetched 14 September 2026.

    Source: Ubersuggest, US, fetched 14 Sep 2026
    14 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/5 min

    How AI engines answer marketing questions: a citation study

    We asked a live AI engine eight marketing questions and checked every source it named. It performed a live search for two of them. Of the sources it listed for the rest, 21 did not exist. This is our own test, run on one engine on one day, and the method and its limits are below.

    Source: Gemini with Google Search grounding, 14 Sep 2026
    14 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/5 min

    What people search for in marketing: the demand map

    We measured US monthly search volume for every topic a marketing reference site might cover. Three findings. Film titles are the largest entries by a wide margin, concept names beat author names by roughly two hundred to one, and two categories we had planned barely exist as searches. Our own data, fetched 14 September 2026.

    Source: Ubersuggest and Virlo, US, fetched 14 Sep 2026
    08

    Movies about advertising

    The shelf we keep for the evening.

    Films and series about advertising, persuasion, brands and the people who sell them. Year, director and what each one is really about.

    All Movies
    5 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    Thank You for Smoking (2005)

    Jason Reitman's satire follows a tobacco lobbyist who can argue any side of any question. It is a film about spin, about the gap between persuasion and truth, and about what happens to a person who is very good at the first and indifferent to the second.

    Source: Directed by Jason Reitman, from the Christopher Buckley novel
    1 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    The Founder (2016)

    Ray Kroc turns a single hamburger stand in San Bernardino into McDonald's. The product was the brothers'. The system was theirs too. The brand, the real estate and the name became his. It is the best film ever made about what a brand actually is, and it is not a flattering answer.

    28 Aug 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    Art & Copy (2009)

    Doug Pray's documentary on the creative revolution in American advertising, with Lee Clow, George Lois, Mary Wells Lawrence, Hal Riney and others talking about the work that defined the trade. It is the most affectionate film on this list and the least critical.

    Source: Directed by Doug Pray, 2009

    Songs

    The hum, set to music.

    Songs that are about advertising, selling, media and wanting things. Not songs used in ads. Songs about the business of it.

    All Songs
    6 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    This Note's for You. Neil Young (1988)

    A refusal to sell songs to sponsors, with a title that parodies a beer slogan. MTV initially declined to air the video, then gave it Video of the Year in 1989. It is the clearest artefact of the moment when musicians had to decide whether sponsorship was selling out.

    Source: Neil Young, Reprise Records, 1988
    2 Sep 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    Sell Out. Reel Big Fish (1996)

    A ska-punk band signs to a major label and writes a hit about signing to a major label. The commercial bargain, sung cheerfully from inside it. It is the funniest entry in this section and the one that best describes how the argument actually ended.

    27 Aug 2026/Anne Becheru, Brave New/3 min

    Television, the Drug of the Nation. The Disposable Heroes of Hiphoprisy (1992)

    Michael Franti's spoken-word indictment of broadcast media, consumption and attention, written before the feed replaced the channel. It is the most analytically serious song in this section and the one that has aged into something closer to prophecy than protest.
